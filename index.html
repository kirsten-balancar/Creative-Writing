Vince Kirsten B. Balgomia

Scientist's mind. Writer's voice. Leader's heart.

Vince Kirsten B. Balgomia, usually called Kirsten, is a Grade 12 HUMSS student currently studying at Judge Feliciano Belmonte Sr. High School.

As a student leader, researcher, and innovator himself, he has developed a strong interest in writing, research, mathematics, science, and the mysteries of the universe. His curiosity often leads him to explore ideas through both analytical thinking and creative expression.

Beyond the classroom, he enjoys connecting the precision of mathematics and science with the imagination of literature. His works reflect an interest in people, society, ideas, truth, and the world beyond what can immediately be seen.

"A curious and ambitious thinker with a scientist's mind, a writer's voice, and a leader's heart."

Rezielyn Pestilos

A Quiet Kind of Love

Love expressed through stillness, trust, and sincerity.

The poem conveys that genuine romance does not rely on grand or performative gestures. Instead, lasting love is portrayed as a conscious daily commitment grounded in kindness, trust, sincerity, and shared stillness.

The Road Only I Could See

A journey through uncertainty, scars, resilience, and hope.

The piece explores personal growth, resilience, and self-discovery through hardship. Darkness represents uncertainty and emotional pain, while the coming morning symbolizes clarity, hope, and the strength gained from every struggle.

Abby Stephanie Sta. Maria

Mountstrain

A call to recognize pollution before nature's beauty is lost.

The poem addresses environmental destruction caused by pollution and plastic waste. Trash becomes an unnatural scar on the environment, leading to an urgent call for humanity to protect nature, speak up, and act.

James Lucero

Admired, Never Possessed

A meditation on distant affection and appreciation.

The piece reflects on the beauty of unrequited or distant affection, comparing the beloved to the ocean, moonlight, and stars. It emphasizes that love does not always require ownership or physical closeness; sometimes, appreciation means simply cherishing someone from afar.
